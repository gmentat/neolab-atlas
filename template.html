Method & sources

Fit reflects how central original model research is to each lab. Core entries have public evidence of work on an original model, architecture, training method, or research artifact. They appear without a fit tag in the table. Mostly covers teams with a broader product focus or a clearly described research program with limited results. Borderline covers labs where original model research is harder to establish from public evidence.

Scope describes the organization, such as a startup, nonprofit, or independent research group. The Atlas also follows some research groups that now operate within larger companies.

How we assess innovation

Each lab has one featured example. The assessment says what the team built or tested, what the public evidence shows, and any limits that affect how to read the result. Company-reported results are attributed to the company.

  • New method · A distinct technical mechanism with a public result. Its benefit may still be limited to the test conditions.
  • Working model or system · The team showed it doing a specific job in a benchmark, demo, or deployment.
  • Research artifact · A model, dataset, benchmark, or tool that others can inspect and build on. A performance advantage may still be unknown.
  • Research direction · A public proposal or training run whose results are still unclear.

The labels describe the contribution supported by the cited evidence. Links point to the featured work, its artifacts, or related research. The labels distinguish training code, inference code, weights, and other artifacts where available.

Team data

Each profile labels what the source counts. Headcounts and employee ranges describe an organization. Team rosters and release bylines count the people listed for a group or project. Dates show when the count was reported or the source was checked. Founder lists link to company, research, or third-party sources.

Small teams includes companies with a reported headcount or employee range up to ten. It also includes named research groups with up to ten people credited on their team roster or featured release. For those groups, the count covers the named contributors.

Comparable staffing and experience data are too sparse to estimate talent density across labs.

Ranking method

The featured research date and founding year sorts apply within fit groups. The research date belongs to the specific example shown in Innovations. It can come from a release, paper, experiment, or model repository. Entries without a documented date or year follow the dated entries in their group.

Each activity sort orders all filtered entries by its own signal. Hugging Face shows 30 days of downloads for one linked model repository. GitHub shows lifetime stars for one linked repository. Vercel averages daily AI Gateway shares for November 11–17 and compares them with November 4–10. The snapshots cover different sets of labs, and the table identifies missing observations and recorded zeros.
