How we assess innovation

Each assessment credits a specific public result: a model, experiment, release, or deployment. We compare the result with the closest documented baseline or prior approach where available. A company-reported result stays attributed to the company. Funding and popularity do not influence the assessment.

  • New method · A distinct technical mechanism with a public result. Its benefit may still be limited to the test conditions.
  • Applied advance · A working model or workflow that demonstrates a useful capability, often by adapting known methods.
  • Research artifact · A model, dataset, benchmark, or tool that others can inspect and build on. A performance advantage may still be unknown.
  • Unproven · A research direction or training run with too little public outcome evidence to estimate the advance.

The labels describe the contribution supported by the cited evidence. Artifact links identify the exact release and what it includes, such as training code, inference code, or weights.

Team data

Team records cite announcements, author credits, competition entries, or public team pages. Dates indicate when the number was reported or the roster was checked. Credits and public rosters cover the people named in that source.

Comparable staffing and experience data are too sparse to estimate talent density across labs.

Ranking method

Ranks apply within the filtered results. Downloads and GitHub stars measure activity around a linked model or project. Gateway share measures activity on one platform. These signals can include repeated activity and have different coverage. They do not measure unique users or lab revenue. When two directory entries share a model account, the publisher gets credit.
